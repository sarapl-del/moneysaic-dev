# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Fintech founders and digital banking teams building regulated financial products. They're technically sophisticated and time-strapped, operating inside compliance constraints (PSD2, GDPR, ISO 27001). They know exactly what they need to build and are frustrated by how long it takes. They arrive skeptical of vendor hype and respond to specificity, not promises.

## Product Purpose

moneysaic delivers a fully designed, compliance-ready module stack — banking, KYC, payments, card issuing — branded as the client's own and live in weeks, not months. The product exists to remove the infrastructure and regulatory build burden from fintech founders, so they can focus on their differentiating product layer. Success means a founder can book a call and walk away believing moneysaic is the fastest credible path to market.

## Brand Personality

Confident, precise, understated. Expert-grade without self-promotion. The product earns trust through specificity (8 weeks, named partners, real compliance standards) not adjectives. Tone: direct, unhurried, technically literate. Closest register: Stripe's discipline plus Lemon Squeezy's warmth, applied to regulated fintech.

## Anti-references

- Generic fintech aesthetics: neon accents, dark-mode-by-default, crypto-adjacent energy
- AI startup sameness: purple gradients, glowing orbs, particle backgrounds, "built for the future" vagueness
- Over-animated SaaS: scroll-triggered everything, gimmicky transitions that distract from content
- Legacy banking: cold blues, stock photography, stiff corporate language

Note: moneysaic uses violet (`#5533ff`) as its brand accent, but as a solid, disciplined color — not as a gradient fill or glow source. The anti-reference is the treatment, not the hue.

## Design Principles

1. **Specificity over superlatives.** Claims land through concrete detail — partner names, week counts, compliance standards — not through adjectives like "powerful" or "seamless."
2. **Show the product, earn the trust.** Mockups and module structure are the proof. Visual design amplifies the product, never substitutes for it.
3. **Restraint is confidence.** Whitespace, tight type hierarchy, and controlled color signal that nothing needs to shout. The work speaks.
4. **Module-logic in the layout.** The modular, stackable nature of the product should be legible in how information is organized — distinct, composable, clear.
5. **Compliance made invisible.** Regulatory depth is real, but the interface should make it feel handled — not overwhelming, not hidden.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Sufficient contrast ratios across all text and interactive elements. No motion required for comprehension; animations are decorative and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No dependency on color alone to convey meaning.
